## Explicit recovery for an expired dispatched draft

A dispatched task keeps the immutable timing brief it was created with. Updating cadence or replanning existing calendar entries does not rewrite that task's deadline. The worker still refuses generation after the task's saved review deadline.

Calendar now offers **Replan overdue draft** only for an expired, unfinished queued/blocked calendar draft. The owner chooses an unused future date, a shared IST posting time for all three platforms, a generation lead (49–336 hours locally or 120–336 hosted), a reason and an explicit cancellation acknowledgement. Generation must still be in the future, and review remains exactly 48 hours before posting. Existing dates are not overwritten.

The authenticated same-origin `calendar-recover` action checks the exact calendar/job/task/source snapshot again inside a transaction. It refuses running, cancelled, completed, leased, safety/completion-blocked or source-revoked tasks; reserved/unknown costs; shared links; and revision, approval or publication history. A stale or duplicate request fails closed. A fresh clock check immediately before writes rejects plans whose generation time passed while waiting for locks or completing validation. Explicit links, planning, dispatch and recovery share the calendar lock; task/source/usage are locked before cancellation so a provider claim or reservation cannot race the safety checks.

Successful recovery cancels only that dormant old task, retains its original calendar/job links, brief, partial artifacts and settled ledger entries, and records the reason and old/new entry IDs in an audit event. It creates a separate source-linked future entry with no content task or job. No provider is called, no work is immediately queued, no settings or scheduler permissions change, and no approvals are copied. Later normal eligible dispatch may queue the new task once under the existing owner opt-in, runtime, consent and budget gates. New output requires fresh content approval and separate publication approval.

Offline verification uses disposable PGlite databases with outbound calls forbidden, including dispatch → deadline expiry → blocked worker → explicit recovery → one later eligible dispatch; stale/concurrent requests; leases; unknown/reserved accounting; source revocation; cancellation; partial-artifact retention; invalid timing; occupied dates; transactional rollback, and clock advancement during lock/validation delays. No schema migration or production data action is needed.
